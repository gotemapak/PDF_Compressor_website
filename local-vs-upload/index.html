Why the Difference Exists

Upload-based PDF compressors are built around a server: your browser sends the file over the internet, the server's software compresses it, and you download the result. That architecture is why these tools need an internet connection for every file, why they often ask you to sign up or log in, and why free tiers typically cap file size and monthly usage - storing and processing other people's files on a server costs money and bandwidth.

PDF Compressor is built differently. The compression logic runs inside the Chrome extension itself, directly in your browser. Your file is read from your computer, compressed on your computer, and saved back to your computer - there's no server in the loop at all. That's what makes it possible to work fully offline after installation, skip accounts entirely, and support files up to 150MB with no monthly cap.

Questions About Local vs. Upload-Based Compression

What does "local processing" mean for a PDF compressor?

It means the compression happens on your own device, inside your browser, instead of on a remote server. Your file is read from your computer, compressed on your computer, and saved back to your computer - it never leaves your device.

How is that different from a typical online PDF compressor?

Most online PDF compressors work by uploading your file to a server, compressing it there, and then letting you download the result. That round trip means your file briefly leaves your control and sits on someone else's infrastructure, and it requires an internet connection for every file.

Does local processing work without an internet connection?

Yes. Because the compression happens on your device rather than a server, PDF Compressor keeps working after installation even without an internet connection. Upload-based compressors need a connection every time, since the file has to reach their server.

Is local processing more private than uploading to a server?

Local processing avoids sending your file to a third party at all, which removes the exposure that comes with any upload - there's nothing to intercept in transit and nothing stored on someone else's server. PDF Compressor also doesn't require an account or sign-up.

Do local and upload-based compressors have different file size limits?

Many free upload-based compressors cap file size well below 100MB and limit how many files you can process per day or month, partly because server storage and bandwidth cost money. PDF Compressor supports files up to 150MB each with no monthly limit, since processing happens on your own device instead of shared server infrastructure.
